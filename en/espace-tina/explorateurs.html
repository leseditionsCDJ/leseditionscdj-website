🌊 No Hiding Place for Sea Turtles!

Sea turtles cannot retract their heads and flippers into their shells.

Their bodies are adapted for swimming, with streamlined shapes and large flippers.

Scientific source: NOAA Fisheries

🐢 A True Giant!

The leatherback sea turtle is the largest living turtle in the world.

It is also the only sea turtle that does not have a hard shell covered with scales like other sea turtles.

Scientific source: NOAA Fisheries

🤿 Diving Champion!

The leatherback sea turtle can dive to about 1,200 metres deep.

NOAA has recorded dives approaching 4,000 feet, or a little over 1,200 metres.

Scientific source: NOAA Fisheries

💧 Salty “Tears”!

Sea turtles have glands near their eyes that help remove excess salt from their bodies.

Out of the water, this secretion can make it look like they are crying.

Scientific source: NOAA

✋ Its Shell Can Feel Touch!

A sea turtle can detect when its shell is touched.

Fine nerves run through the shell and detect changes in pressure caused by contact.

Scientific source: Smithsonian Ocean

👂 Loggerheads Can Hear Underwater!

Studies have shown that hatchling and young loggerhead sea turtles can hear and respond to low-frequency sounds.

Their ears are hidden beneath a layer of skin.

Scientific source: Smithsonian Ocean

🌙 Light Shows the Way!

Loggerhead hatchlings use the brightness of the horizon to find the ocean after leaving the nest.

On a natural beach, the horizon over the sea is brighter than the area near the dunes. Artificial lights can unfortunately disorient them.

Scientific source: NOAA Fisheries

🌊 Years in the Open Ocean!

Young loggerhead sea turtles generally spend their first 7 to 15 years in the open ocean.

They then move to areas closer to shore to continue growing.

Scientific source: NOAA Fisheries

😮 A Very Special Way of Breathing!

The Mary River turtle, in Australia, can absorb oxygen through the surface of its cloaca.

This unusual way of absorbing oxygen is called cloacal respiration.

Scientific source: Australian Museum

🎨 A Shell Almost Like a Fingerprint!

In Eastern box turtles, the colourful patterns on the shell are unique to each individual.

Smithsonian researchers compare them to our fingerprints.

Scientific source: Smithsonian's National Zoo
