🏆 A giant among rodents!

The North American beaver is the largest rodent in North America.

An adult can weigh several dozen pounds.

Source: National Park Service

🦷 Its teeth never stop growing!

A beaver's large front teeth, called incisors, grow continuously throughout its life.

The front of the teeth has strong orange-colored enamel. Their special structure helps keep the teeth sharp while the beaver gnaws.

Source: National Park Service

🤿 A diving champion!

A beaver can stay underwater for up to about 15 minutes.

This helps it travel, carry materials and reach the underwater entrance to its lodge.

Source: National Park Service

👀 Built-in swimming goggles!

Beavers have a transparent membrane that protects their eyes underwater.

They can also close their nostrils and ears when they dive.

Source: National Park Service

🏊 A tail with many talents!

A beaver's large flat tail helps it steer while swimming.

It can also slap the surface of the water to warn other beavers when danger is nearby.

Source: National Park Service

🌳 It doesn't really eat the hard wood!

Beavers often chew trees, but they prefer the softer plant tissue underneath the bark.

This layer is called the cambium. Beavers also eat leaves, twigs and aquatic plants.

Source: National Park Service

🏠 The front door is underwater!

Beaver lodges can have entrances located below the surface of the water.

The living area inside remains dry, while the underwater entrance helps protect the family from predators.

Source: National Park Service

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Young beavers stay with the family!

Young beavers can remain with their parents for about two years.

During that time, they live as part of the family colony.

Source: National Park Service

🌿 An engineer of nature!

Beavers are often called ecosystem engineers.

Their dams can create wetlands that provide habitat for many other plants and animals.

Source: National Park Service

🌲 A pond can become a meadow!

When an old beaver dam is abandoned, the pond may slowly fill with sediment and vegetation.

Over time, the old pond can become a meadow and continue changing as the ecosystem develops.

Source: National Park Service
